Footprint in real brand colours

Every colour here is copied from a real brand: NASA's and NATO's published guidelines, Apple's system colours from its design guidelines, and the colours inside Telegram's and Signal's own logo files and code. Nothing is mixed or sampled. The shape is unchanged.

What the guidelines say, and what went wrong last time:

  • Apple, app icons: Avoid using black for your icon's background. Lighten a black background so the icon doesn't blend into the display background. Most of the dull options had black tiles.
  • Apple, app icons: Design a background that both stands out and emphasizes foreground content, and prefer a simple background, such as a solid color or gradient. So: one strong brand colour, and white for the subject.
  • Web accessibility rule for shapes (WCAG 2.1, 1.4.11): parts of a graphic need at least 3:1 contrast. The ocean globe on black last round was 1.8:1, which is why it looked muddy. Each option below lists its contrast; numbers under 3 are dotted.

My pick

NASA, insignia order

The colours in the order NASA's insignia uses them: a blue disc, white drawn on it, one red accent. The footprint becomes a white patch of light on a blue Earth, and the red point is Munich. Strongest contrast of the set at every size.

On a light taskbar the white tile blends in (the blue globe still stands out, as with any white-tile app icon). It borrows NASA's look; for a private tool that is homage, not a problem.

  • NASA Blue #0032A0 globe
  • NASA Red #E4002B pupil: Munich
  • White #FFFFFF tile and footprint

Source: NASA brand guidelines: insignia colours. NASA Blue PMS 286 #0032A0, NASA Red PMS 185 #E4002B, read from NASA's colour chart (round one).

Recommendation

NASA, insignia order

Real colours with a real meaning, the strongest contrast of the set (10.7:1 between globe and tile, 4.8:1 for the red point), and it stops looking like a cartoon eye: the white footprint reads as light on the Earth, not as an eyeball. Apple's Indigo and Blue is the choice if you want it to sit naturally beside Apple's own icons.

Questions for you

  1. Which one? Mixing is fine, for example NASA's colours for the icon and Apple's blue for buttons in the app.
  2. Should the app's interface take the chosen colours (buttons, selection, accents), or keep its current ice blue?
